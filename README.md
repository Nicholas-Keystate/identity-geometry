# Identity geometry

The study of committed predicates over identifiers: their domains,
their competence boundaries, and how tiers of authority compose at
the questions each tier declines to answer.

This repository is the science's kernel — the object definitions,
the composition law, and the mathematical spine — held deliberately
minimal. It is not a theory of everything about identity. It is the
smallest committed vocabulary that lets four running artifacts cite
one another precisely, published because a science whose kernel
lives in one person's working notes fails the property the science
itself is about: that a stranger can check.

## The one-paragraph statement

An identifier is never predicate-free. The cheapest KERI AID
already carries committed predicates — duplicity detection,
pre-rotation, recovery geometry — minted by the protocol itself.
Every enrichment up the ladder (witnessing, delegation, chartered
organs, governed domains) adds committed predicates over the same
kind of thing. Identity geometry types this whole ladder with six
objects and one law. The six objects: the **mint** (a predicate
with a domain and a stated boundary), the **domain** (what
predicates range over), the **seam** (the question a predicate
declines to evaluate), the **evaluation point** (a question that
can be asked of a predicate), the **mint event** (the provenance
of a predicate's fixing), and the **binding** (an instantiation of
a predicate by an engine, or of a registry by a reader). The one
law: **tiers compose at seams** — what one tier of authority
refuses to answer is exactly the domain of the tier above it.

## Authority

This kernel is descriptive. It charts predicates that other
artifacts commit — the KERI protocol's own mints, the Custos
standard's fold tower, the anchor registry's custody chain — and
misreading any of them is a defect of this repository, convictable
against the cited bytes. Where the kernel states a law of its own
(the composition law, the evaluation law), that law binds only the
kernel's consumers by their own citation of it, at the revision
they pin.

The two-move discipline governs scope: the kernel grows only when
a consumer needs an object it lacks. There is no roadmap toward
completeness. A complete geometry of identity is a limit object;
this repository is a committed fragment, and stays one.
