# The six kinds

Settled in the ontology sittings of 2026-08-07/08 (three rounds of
adversarial dialogue; the pressure points each object survived are
listed with it, because an object that has never been pushed on is
a de-facto axiom of the schema itself).

## mint

A committed predicate: a domain Σ it ranges over, a computable
f: Σ → {sat, unsat}, and a seam. The triple is the unit of
normative content — every normative sentence in a governed
standard is a mint or part of one.

- Σ is declared, never inferred. A predicate whose domain must be
  guessed is not yet a mint.
- f returns sat or unsat over Σ, and nothing over anything else.
  Refusal is not a third value (see seam).
- **Pressure point (survived):** are reflexive laws mints? A
  document's laws about its own corpus (append-never, a
  comprehension gate) are mints whose Σ is the corpus itself —
  reflexive Σ is admitted honestly rather than exiled to a
  special kind.
- **Grounds are root mints.** A ground is not a separate kind: it
  is a mint at which derives-from chains terminate — a commitment
  you cannot dig beneath (a genesis knot, a protocol's own
  predicates, a document-system's reflexive laws). Teaching prose
  is not a ground and does not enter a registry; it excises to
  companions. (Pressure point: an earlier draft typed "ground" as
  narrative material and collided with committed vocabulary —
  "grounding up, governing down." The collision was caught by the
  coinage's owner and the restructure is this paragraph.)

## domain

A first-class Σ-construction: what predicates range over. Two
realizations of one kind: a **description** (a spec passage that
constructs a type — postures, object forms, event classes) and an
**instance** (an identifier under committed law).

- **The ladder.** No AID is predicate-free — the protocol itself
  mints (duplicity detection, pre-rotation, recovery geometry are
  committed predicates of the medium). The enrichment ladder runs:
  vanilla AID ⊂ witnessed ⊂ delegated ⊂ seated organ ⊂ governed
  domain ⊂ registry-as-office — degrees of predicate enrichment
  over ONE kind, never different species.
- Domains are extensible upward by construction: the ladder's top
  is wherever commitment has currently reached, not a closed enum.
- **Pressure point (survived):** shared domains are the
  load-bearing walls of a corpus — a Σ-construction consumed by
  many mints must be visible as a node, or re-rooting analysis
  goes blind. This is why domains are first-class rather than
  attributes of mints.

## seam

A committed competence boundary: the question a predicate
**declines to evaluate**. Refusal at a seam means "this question
is not mine to answer" — distinct in kind from "the answer is no,"
and never a third truth value.

- A seam is a jurisdiction statement, not an enforcement act.
- Seams are where silent overclaim dies: a predicate that quietly
  evaluates what it does not range over is the root defect class
  (a type argument answering a transport question it had no
  competence over). The seam declaration makes the predicate's
  silence loud.
- **Rendering law (ruled at first projection):** a seam renders as
  QUESTION + ROUTING ("answered by: tier N / open"), never as a
  declaration — declarative rendering makes boundary statements
  read as axioms, which was the first projection's first defect.

## evaluation point

A coordinate (σ, f): a question that can be asked of a predicate.
NOT a value-in-waiting.

- f(σ) may be mathematically determined by the committed bytes,
  but **determination without evaluation produces no testimony** —
  until some fold computes the value, it binds nothing, convicts
  nothing, grounds nothing. (The committed answer testifies only
  when computed.)
- **A point with no recorded evaluation is a de-facto axiom**: it
  sits in every consumer's trust posture on standing, not on
  check. A conformance corpus is the systematic demotion of
  de-facto axioms into verified claims. Coverage — which regions
  of a domain have been heard — is a computed property of a
  registry, never an impression.

## mint event

Provenance: the committed act that fixed a predicate's f — a
ruling, an adjudication, a ratification. Edges from mints to their
mint events answer "why is this law" with a citation instead of a
story.

## binding

A β: an instantiation. Two faces of one kind:

- **Engine bindings** — an implementation computing a mint's f.
  All divergence between computations lives in bindings; the mint
  never contains its instantiations. Comparison is β-to-β at the
  resolution the shared mint has fixed (byte-grade under one
  pinned serialization; semantic-grade across engines until an
  encoding ratifies).
- **Reader bindings** — a human-facing projection of a registry
  (prose, figures, overviews). Drift between a projection and its
  registry is binding divergence, convictable against the
  registry — never a matter of editorial taste.

The binding's citation discipline is era-graded; see
`kernel/evaluation.md`.

## What is deliberately absent

- **No stratum taxonomy.** Positions (root, interior, shared-wall,
  leaf) are computed from the graph; they receive names only when
  a consumer needs to cite them and a discriminating membership
  test exists. A taxonomy without a test is manufactured
  structure. (Pressure point: a five-way stratum enum was proposed
  and convicted — untested, unjustified, comfortable. This
  paragraph is its tombstone.)
- **No named instances.** The kernel types kinds; exhibits live in
  consumers and survive deletion.
